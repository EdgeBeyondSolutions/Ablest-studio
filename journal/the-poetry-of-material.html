Materials

The Poetry of Material

Material is rarely a neutral decision. Concrete reads as permanence; black cherry wood reads as warmth; rammed earth reads as belonging to a site rather than sitting on top of it. Before a material performs structurally, it performs emotionally — it tells a visitor what kind of space they're about to enter.

In Casa Jacaranda, that idea took the form of a palette rather than a single gesture: marfil cálido, arena, terracota, arcilla, café tabaco, nogal, and verde olivo, moved across three rooms without a full remodel. The palette itself became the design tool — proof that materiality doesn't require demolition, only intention.

The same logic runs through Salvia Modern House Museum, where concrete, black cherry wood, dwarf yaupon, and glass are layered to mark a gradual transition from public to private as a visitor moves up through the building. Material sequencing became spatial sequencing.
