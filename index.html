Ablest Studio is the independent practice of Aldo Ballesteros — biophilic, materially grounded design work spanning residential interiors, cultural buildings, and academic research.

About

I am Aldo Ballesteros, an architecture student and emerging designer passionate about creating spaces that balance functionality, aesthetics, and human experience. My work is driven by a curiosity for how people interact with the built environment and how thoughtful design can enhance everyday life. Through academic projects, professional experience, and continuous exploration, I strive to develop architecture that is both meaningful and enduring.

I am particularly interested in residential architecture, sustainable design, and the relationship between architecture and landscape. Inspired by natural forms, materials, and the unique character of each site, I approach every project as an opportunity to create spaces that are purposeful, elegant, and deeply connected to their surroundings. My portfolio represents my growth as a designer and my commitment to crafting environments that positively impact the people who experience them.

Sophomore Year — Spring 2026

Salvia Modern House Museum

337 W Commerce St, San Antonio, TX 78205

A biomimetic design inspired by Salvia farinacea, stacking public program over private retreat: lobby and café, two art galleries, a community hub, two artist's lofts, and a rooftop vinothèque. The building is organized as a stacked spatial sequence — each level a gradual transition from public to private — built from concrete, black cherry wood, dwarf yaupon, and glass. Its form and systems are shaped throughout by COTE guidelines for integration, community, ecology, water, and energy: a hybrid passive-and-mechanical ventilation strategy using stack effect and wind-driven exhaust, a 1,500 sf roof harvesting roughly 29,900 gallons of water a year, and a 15kW PV system generating close to 22,000 kWh annually.

Program — galleries, lofts, community hub, vinothèque Materials — concrete, black cherry, dwarf yaupon, glass Strategy — COTE / passive ventilation, rainwater, PV
Architecture at Zero 2026 — Student Division

La Raíz (The Root)

Antelope Valley Fair & Event Center, Lancaster, CA

A 10,000 sf, two-story, all-electric biophilic cultural center and emergency operations center for the Antelope Valley. Two programs share one building: "La Plaza Viva," for everyday cultural life, and "Centro de Mando," for emergency response. Rammed earth walls sit beneath an arched bifacial photovoltaic canopy, targeting a net-negative energy use intensity of −1 kBtu/sf·yr. The proposal is grounded in an equity essay tying the design to Lancaster's Latino community.

Size — 10,000 sf Target — Net EUI −1 kBtu/sf·yr Structure — rammed earth + PV canopy
Sophomore Year — Fall 2025

Aquilo Flow

A research space drawn from the Edwards Aquifer — its movement, its scarcity, its mineral identity of calcium and magnesium — translated into flow, layering, and filtered light. A long, linear, slatted pavilion sits on a terraced site above a black resin pool, framed by plantings of dried baby's breath.

Site — terraced, water-adjacent Form — linear slatted pavilion
Interior Design — 2026

Casa Jacaranda

An interior reimagining for a client who wanted her residence to reflect contemporary Mexican identity without a full remodel — achieved through furniture, lighting, textiles, and artesanía, drawing on contemporary Mexico City residential design. The palette moves through marfil cálido, arena, terracota, arcilla, café tabaco, nogal, and verde olivo across three documented rooms: Sala entre Muros, Cocina Raíz, and Comedor Raíz.

Scope — furniture, lighting, textiles Rooms — sala, cocina, comedor
